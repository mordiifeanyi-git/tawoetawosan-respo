It is a familiar scene in many Nigerian workplaces. A worker arrives late, misses a shift or falls foul of a workplace rule, and the next payslip is lighter than it ought to be. Employers often regard such a reduction as a natural and proportionate sanction. The Labour Act (Cap. L1, Laws of the Federation of Nigeria 2004) takes a different view. It treats a worker's wages as a protected entitlement, and it does not place the payslip among the instruments of discipline available to an employer.

The starting point is section 5(1)^!. It provides that, unless the Act or some other law expressly permits it, an employer shall not make any deduction from a worker's wages, nor enter into any agreement or contract with the worker for such a deduction, or for any payment by the worker to the employer, in respect of any fines. A reduction in pay imposed as punishment is a fine in everything but name, and describing it as a penalty, a surcharge or an administrative adjustment does not alter its character. Nor can an employer cure the difficulty by inserting a fines clause in the contract of employment, the staff handbook or a signed undertaking, since the section is directed at the agreement as much as at the deduction itself.

The Act does recognise one narrow exception, found in the proviso to section 5(1)^2. Where the employer has suffered injury or loss through the wilful misconduct or neglect of the worker, a reasonable deduction may be made, but only with the prior written consent of an authorised labour officer. The conditions are demanding, and each of them matters. The deduction must be reasonable in amount; the loss must flow from wilful misconduct or neglect, which is a higher threshold than ordinary error, a missed target or general underperformance; and the labour officer's consent must be obtained before any money is withheld. An employer cannot settle the figure alone, and cannot deduct first and regularise the position afterwards.

The other deductions the Act permits are of a different kind altogether, because they are not punitive. With the worker's consent, contributions to approved provident or pension schemes may be deducted, and union dues may be deducted in the manner the Trade Unions Act provides. Overpaid wages may likewise be recovered, though only in respect of overpayments made during the three months immediately preceding the month in which the overpayment was discovered. Whatever the ground for a deduction, the Labour act limits the total that may be made in any one month to one third of the worker's wages for that month.

The consequences of getting this wrong are not merely theoretical. An employer who makes a deduction contrary to Part I of the Act commits an offence under section 21, punishable on conviction by a fine. The sums stated in the Act, ₦800 for a first offence and ₦1,500 for a second or subsequent offence, are modest by present-day standards, but the criminal exposure is only part of the picture. A worker whose wages have been unlawfully withheld may pursue the sums as a claim, and a dispute of this kind rarely improves the relationship between the parties.

None of this leaves an employer without recourse. Discipline remains available through warnings, through suspension in accordance with the contract and the Act, and through termination on due notice or as the contract provides. Indeed, the Act itself contemplates suspension as a form of punishment as a further reading of the Labour Act highlights that the employer's duty to pay wages for days on which work is not provided does not apply where a worker is suspended as a punishment for a breach of discipline. The distinction is one of principle. An employer may discipline a worker, but may not do so by means of an unlawful deduction.

A word on scope is also necessary. The Act's definition of "worker" excludes certain categories of person, including those exercising administrative, executive, technical or professional functions. Whether the protections discussed above apply will therefore depend on the worker's actual role, and that question should be considered carefully in any particular case.
