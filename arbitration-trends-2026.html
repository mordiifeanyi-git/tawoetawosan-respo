The Evolution of Commercial Arbitration and Alternative Dispute Resolution in Nigeria

By Senior Practice Group • Tawo E. Tawo, SAN & Co.

As commercial transactions expand in complexity and cross-border volume, traditional court-based litigation often faces severe structural delays. Consequently, corporate entities and commercial enterprises are increasingly turning to institutional arbitration as the preferred avenue for swift and confidential conflict resolution.

1. The Shift Toward Specialized Arbitration Frameworks

Modern commercial contracts in sectors such as energy, telecommunications, and finance frequently feature robust multi-tiered dispute resolution clauses. Rather than immediately resorting to litigation, parties prioritize mediation and conciliation, escalating to binding arbitration only when preliminary negotiations break down. This structured approach preserves commercial relationships while securing enforceable outcomes.

2. Judicial Support and Enforcement of Arbitral Awards

A core pillar of a thriving arbitration ecosystem is the judiciary's willingness to respect and enforce arbitral awards without unwarranted intervention. Superior courts in Nigeria have consistently demonstrated a pro-arbitration stance, strictly limiting grounds for setting aside awards. This judicial posture instills tremendous confidence in foreign and domestic investors who require certainty and legal predictability.

3. Confidentiality and Commercial Efficiency

Unlike public court proceedings where sensitive proprietary information can become a matter of public record, arbitration proceedings remain strictly confidential. For corporate boards managing high-stakes disputes, this confidentiality prevents adverse publicity, protects intellectual property, and shields corporate valuations during active proceedings.
